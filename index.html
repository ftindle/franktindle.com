What I’m useful for

The things that break when a business changes shape.

Acquisitions, and what comes after

Three acquisitions, three integrations, across three decades. Acclaim’s purchase of Iguana in 1995, where I unified email across New York, Austin, Utah and the UK. EA’s £40M acquisition of Criterion in 2004, where Guildford, Paris, Austin and Tokyo folded into EA’s infrastructure, 300+ people, zero disruption to game releases. Then Supermassive into Nordisk Games in 2022. The technical half is the easy half. The rest is people deciding whether to stay.

AI in the back office

Founded a cross-functional group spanning operations, HR, IT and studio management to take the grind out of routine work. Started as four people comparing notes and became the way the back office thinks about this. The useful wins here are unglamorous and that’s the point: the reporting, the ticket triage, the admin that quietly eats a week a month.

Scaling the operation

Joined a 30-person work-for-hire studio as its only IT manager. Built the operational side of the business from a standing start to a team of 16, covering IT, workplace, facilities, physical and cyber security, events and procurement, then carried it to a multi-site group that peaked at 387 people, and through acquisition by Nordisk Games. Sat on the Senior Leadership Team.

Growth in reverse

Then the industry turned, as it has right across the sector. I ran the estate and the function through a sustained contraction: sites consolidated, run rate cut, workforce reductions handled directly and decently, people absorbed from departments that were closing, service held up throughout. Scaling down well is rarer than scaling up, and a good deal harder to do properly.

Buildings, and keeping the lights on

Took a studio from 14,000 sq ft into 40,000 across two buildings with nobody losing a working day. Earlier, part of the leadership team on a 96,000 sq ft fit-out consolidating three EA sites into one. And on the way out of a building, took the dilapidations works in-house rather than accepting the landlord’s contractor, and delivered them well under the schedule.

Background

Thirty-one years in operations, most of it in games, across multiple BAFTA-winning studios. Sixteen at Supermassive Games as IT & Operations Director, before that Criterion through the Burnout years and the EA acquisition, and before that Acclaim as Director of Operations.

Running the operational half of a business. I build good teams and get out of their way. No fuss, no jargon. Let people focus on the work and nothing else.
